Omar Ali Saifuddien Mosque

Completed in 1958, and named after Sultan Omar Ali Saifuddien III, this mosque is one of Brunei's best-known landmarks and a symbol of the country.

It stands on the edge of a lagoon, with its gold-covered dome rising above the water. A replica of a 16th-century royal barge is also located nearby in the lagoon, and it's worth a look even if you don't go inside.

Visitors are welcome, including non-Muslims, but entry is limited to set times outside the prayer periods, and modest dress is required. Silence is expected inside the mosque at all times, shoes must be taken off and robes will be offered for visitors to wear whilst inside the mosque. Opening times can change, so check current schedule on Visit Southeast Asia's mosque guide. before you go.

Kampong Ayer

Kampong Ayer, also known as the "Venice of the East," has been inhabited for around 1,300 years. Today, roughly around 9,000 people still live there.

Visitors can have a walking tour through the village, trying local food and snacks like prawn crackers and kuih, and watching craftspeople at work.

In order to get there, you can take water taxis from the Royal Wharf to Jetty 1, which is just beside the Cultural and Tourism Gallery and it is used as a form of transportation for both locals and tourists. Boat operators also run tours from here to the nearby mangrove forests to catch a glimpse of the wildlife and the natural beauty of the area such as proboscis monkeys, monitor lizards and crocodiles.

Ulu Temburong National Park

The crown jewel of Brunei's green landscape, Ulu Temburong National Park, is located at the easternmost part of Brunei in the Temburong District.

The main reason to come visit is the canopy walkway, which lifts you above the forest floor and gives visitors a unique experience to observe the rainforest's treetops. It's a rare view of a rainforest from the top down.

To get there, it takes approximately 2 hours to get to the park. The journey starts with a 45-minute speed boat ride from Bandar Seri Begawan to a small town called Bangar. From there, you take a short overland journey to Batang Duri. The final stretch is a 30-minute longboat ride. For current details, see the Brunei Tourism website for Ulu Temburong.

Jerudong Park

Jerudong Park, in the Brunei-Muara District, is described by Brunei Tourism as the largest amusement park in Borneo with many rides and attractions to explore all around. The park having rides for all ages including a kids waterpark and some more thrilling rides, there is also an outdoor foodcourt for when you go hungry.

It suits a relaxed family day out with plenty of activities for all ages. The park does not open every day, and its hours change through the week, so check the Official Brunei Tourism page for Jerudong Park for the current opening times and entrance fees before you go.

Royal Regalia Museum

The Royal Regalia Museum in Bandar Seri Begawan was officially opened on 30 September 1992 as part of the celebrations marking 25 years of the Sultan's reign. It is dedicated to the regalia and royal heritage of Brunei.

Inside, four galleries tell the story in different ways. One covers the 1968 coronation, with the royal chariot used in the procession through the capital. Another follows the Sultan's life from childhood to the coronation, and two more are devoted to the Silver and Golden Jubilees.

The museum is closed on Fridays. Non-residents pay an entrance fee, so check the Official Brunei Tourism page for the Royal Regalia Museumfor current hours and prices before you go.
