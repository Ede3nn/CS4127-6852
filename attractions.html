Omar Ali Saifuddien Mosque

Completed in 1958, and named after Sultan Omar Ali Saifuddien III, this mosque is one of Brunei's best-known landmarks and a symbol of the country.

It stands on the edge of a lagoon, with its gold-covered dome rising above the water. A replica of a 16th-century royal barge is also located nearby in the lagoon, and it's worth a look even if you don't go inside.

Visitors are welcome, including non-Muslims, but entry is limited to set times outside the prayer periods, and modest dress is required. Silence is expected inside the mosque at all times, shoes must be taken off and robes will be offered for visitors to wear whilst inside the mosque. Opening times can change, so check current schedule on Visit Southeast Asia's mosque guide. before you go.

Kampong Ayer

Kampong Ayer, also known as the "Venice of the East," has been inhabited for around 1,300 years. Today, roughly around 9,000 people still live there.

Visitors can have a walking tour through the village, trying local food and snacks like prawn crackers and kuih, and watching craftspeople at work.

In order to get there, you can take water taxis from the Royal Wharf to Jetty 1, which is just beside the Cultural and Tourism Gallery and it is used as a form of transportation for both locals and tourists. Boat operators also run tours from here to the nearby mangrove forests to catch a glimpse of the wildlife and the natural beauty of the area such as proboscis monkeys, monitor lizards and crocodiles.

Ulu Temburong National Park

Explore the pristine rainforests and diverse wildlife of Ulu Temburong National Park, one of Brunei's most breathtaking natural wonders.

Jerudong Park

Enjoy the lush greenery and recreational facilities at Jerudong Park, a popular destination for both locals and tourists.

Royal Regalia Museum

Discover the rich history and culture of Brunei at the Royal Regalia Museum, which houses a collection of royal artifacts and exhibits.
