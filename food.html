Local Food and Dining

What to eat

Ambuyat

Brunei's national dish is a sticky starch made from the trunk of the sago palm. Diners twirl it around a bamboo fork called a 'chandas' and dip it in a tangy sauce.

Nasi Katok

Rice with fried chicken and a spoonful of sambal, often wrapped in paper to take away. It is one of the cheapest local meals you can find.

Pulut panggang and kuih

Pulut panggang is filled glutinous rice wrapped in banana leaf and grilled. Kuih are small sweet snacks, and night markets are a good place to find both.

Source: Brunei Tourism: Local Delicacies

Where to eat

Night markets and local restaurants are the easiest places to try these dishes.

Halal and Dietary Information

Halal Food

Food in Brunei is prepared to halal standards, so Muslim visitors can eat with confidence. Check the Ministry of Religious Affairs page below for how halal certification works.

Dietary Restrictions

Alcohol is not sold, and pork is avoided in most restaurants. If you are a vegetarian, vegan or have other needs, ask staff about ingredients and how the food is cooked.

Food Allergies

Tell restaurant staff about any allergies before you order, including nuts, dairy, seafood and gluten, and ask about cross-contamination.
