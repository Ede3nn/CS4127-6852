Local customs

Brunei is a Malay Islamic Monarchy. Dress modestly, remove shoes before entering homes and mosques, and use your right hand to give or receive things. Alcohol is not sold in shops and restaurants.

What to eat

Ambuyat

A type of starch derived from the trunk of a sago palm and cooked as a delicacy in the country of Brunei. It is consumed with a two-pronged bamboo utensil known as 'chandas'.

Nasi Katok

Nasi Katok is a simple yet popular dish consisting of rice, fried chicken, and sambal (spicy chili paste). It is often served in a banana leaf or wrapped in paper for takeaway.

Pulut Panggang and Kueh

Pulut Panggang is a traditional snack that is prepared by wrapping its contents in banana leaves and grilled instead of steaming, and sweet snacks you will see in night markets.

Halal and Dietary Information

Brunei Darussalam is a Muslim-majority country where halal food is widely available. The country's food industry follows Islamic dietary principles, making it a convenient destination for Muslim travellers.

Halal Food Availability

Many restaurants and food establishments in Brunei cater to Muslim dietary requirements. Food businesses are subject to Brunei's halal certification requirements, regulated by the Ministry of Religious Affairs.

Visitors can look for official Brunei halal certification when choosing restaurants and food products.

Dietary Restrictions

Pork and its derivatives are prohibited under Islamic dietary laws. Alcohol is also prohibited for Muslims. Visitors with vegetarian, vegan or other dietary requirements should check ingredients and preparation methods with restaurants.

Food Allergies and Special Diets

Travellers with food allergies or special dietary needs should inform restaurant staff before ordering. It is advisable to check for ingredients such as nuts, dairy, seafood and gluten, as well as possible cross-contimination.
